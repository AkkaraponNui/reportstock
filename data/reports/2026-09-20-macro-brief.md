# Macro and Sector Brief — reportstock Watchlist
**As of 2026-09-20. Five-year horizon (through roughly 2031).**

Source base: `uv run python tools/fetch_news.py --macro --days 45` (118 articles,
run 2026-09-20, saved to `data/news/_macro/2026-09-20.json`), plus targeted
WebSearch on 2026-09-20 for facts the sweep only gestured at. Every factual claim
below carries a publisher and a date. Forecasts are labelled as mine.

## The two or three forces that matter most

**1. The Fed just started hiking, not cutting, and the long end is at its
highest level since 2007.** The FOMC raised the federal funds target to
3.75%-4.00% on 2026-09-16, a 12-0 vote and the first hike since 2023, with
Chair Kevin Warsh calling inflation "too high... for too long" and 16 of 18
officials penciling in at least one more hike this year ([CNBC,
2026-09-16](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html);
[Chase, 2026-09-16](https://www.chase.com/personal/investments/learning-and-insights/article/federal-reserve-raises-rates-officials-signal-one-more-hike-in-2026)).
August CPI ran 3.4% y/y, above forecast ([Morningstar/CNBC/Bloomberg,
2026-09-11](https://www.morningstar.com)). The 10-year Treasury yield hit
5.00%-5.04% around the decision, its highest since 2007, up from a run that
started well below that level ([CNBC, 2026-09-08](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/us-treasury-yields-bonds.html);
[MacroRadar, accessed 2026-09-20](https://www.macroradar.io/10-year-treasury-yield)).
This is a discount-rate shock, not a rounding error. A ~100bp move in the
long rate compresses the present value of cash flows furthest in the future
hardest. In this watchlist that is SNOW, ARM, ENPH, and TSLA (thin or
negative current profitability, most of the equity value modeled beyond year
five), and to a lesser extent NOW and CRM. It is close to a non-event for
JPM (higher rates widen net interest margin, a partial offset), V and MA
(fee-on-volume models with short cash-flow duration), and BRK-B (a large net
cash position that now earns more). **What would change this read:** a
downside inflation surprise in the September or October CPI print that lets
the Fed skip the "one more hike," or a 10-year yield that falls back under
4.5% on its own.

**2. The AI capital-spending cycle is now running ahead of hyperscaler cash
flow, and it is being funded increasingly with debt just as debt got more
expensive.** Bank of America (cited via Fool.com, 2026-09-06) projects
hyperscaler capex near $860bn in 2026 rising toward $1.2tn in 2027, with
aggregate free cash flow across the group swinging from +$180bn (2025) to
roughly -$64bn (2026), -$144bn (2027), and -$186bn (2028). Alphabet's free
cash flow turned negative in a quarter for the first time since its 2004 IPO
([Fool.com, 2026-09-06](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/06/hyperscalers-driving-ai-capex-cash-flow/)).
The gap is being filled with financing: Nvidia disclosed over $540bn in
financing arrangements during 2026, and outlets are now describing a roughly
$745bn Nvidia-OpenAI-Oracle circular financing loop that a September 2026
piece explicitly frames as having just hit "its first stress test" in the
form of the Fed's rate hike ([Inkl,
2026-09-2026](https://www.inkl.com/news/nvidia-openai-and-oracles-745b-financing-circle-just-hit-its-first-stress-test-a-fed-rate-hike);
Nvidia also announced financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone,
Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR aiming to mobilize $500bn+ for AI
infrastructure). This helps the chip and equipment supply chain (NVDA, AMD,
AVGO, TSM, ASML, AMAT, MU, ARM) as long as orders keep flowing, and it
pressures the balance sheets and capital discipline of the spenders (MSFT,
GOOGL, AMZN, META) whose own free cash flow is being consumed rather than
returned. It is a tailwind for the physical-infrastructure names (VRT, ETN)
regardless of which chip vendor wins, since they sell into the buildout
itself. **What would falsify this being a live risk:** hyperscaler capex
guidance actually decelerating in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 earnings calls, or
credit spreads on AI-linked debt widening in a way that shows up in
financing costs rather than staying theoretical.

**3. Chip export controls to China flipped from a ban to a taxed, capped,
case-by-case channel, and geopolitics on both energy and minerals is now
directly in the inflation print.** A 2026-01-14 proclamation imposed a 25%
tariff on advanced computing chips (the H200/MI325X performance class)
exported to China, with a 2026-01-15 BIS rule moving license review from
presumption-of-denial to case-by-case, a 50% volume cap, and KYC/third-party
testing requirements ([BIS,
2026-01-15](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-revises-license-review-policy-semiconductors-exported-china)).
Roughly ten Chinese firms including Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance were
cleared to buy H200s (75,000-unit-per-customer cap), but as of
2026-08-19 no deliveries had actually happened amid legal limbo and Chinese
countermeasures ([CNBC,
2026-08-19](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/china-ai-nvidia-chips-us-export-controls.html)).
Separately, China imposed new export measures on a key chipmaking material,
prompting a Japanese protest on 2026-09-08 ([ABC News,
2026-09-08](https://abcnews.go.com)), and Middle East attacks pushed Brent
above $100-106/bbl in early September 2026 with shipping through the Strait
of Hormuz reportedly halted for most traffic ([Washington Times,
2026-09-09](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/sep/9/oil-climbs-past-100-barrel-latest-wave-middle-east-attacks/);
[CNBC, 2026-09-08](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-iran-war.html)).
Net effect: NVDA and AMD get a legal (if taxed and capped) path back into
China that did not exist earlier in 2026, which is a modest positive versus
outright prohibition but a real tax on the margin of every China unit sold;
TSM and ASML sit further from the controls (foundry and lithography, not the
end customer) but are exposed to any further tightening on tool sales; the
Hormuz disruption is an energy-cost and inflation risk that touches
everything indirectly by keeping the Fed hawkish, and directly raises input
and logistics costs for CAT and 7203.T (Toyota). **What would change this
read:** actual H200 deliveries to Chinese buyers occurring (currently stalled
per CNBC), or a Hormuz de-escalation that lets oil settle back under $80.

## Other forces worth naming

**Drug pricing policy (MFN) — priced but with an offsetting volume story.**
Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk signed most-favored-nation deals with the Trump
administration cutting direct-to-consumer GLP-1 pricing to $350/month (from
$1,000-$1,350) and Medicare/Medicaid pricing to about $245/month, while
Medicare covers obesity treatment for the first time via a Section 402
demonstration beginning 2026-07-01, and all 50 states signed onto a Medicaid
MFN plan as of 2026-09-18 ([Pharmacy Times,
2026](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-strike-mfn-deals-with-trump-administration-to-lower-glp-1-prices);
[US News/Semafor,
2026-09-18](https://www.usnews.com/news/us/articles/2026-09-18/us-plans-medicaid-most-favored-nation-pricing-for-some-drugs-in-all-states-semafor-reports)).
This cuts per-script revenue for LLY and NVO by roughly two-thirds at the
list-price level but simultaneously opens a federal payer channel
(Medicare/Medicaid) that did not previously reimburse obesity drugs at
scale. Whether the volume expansion offsets the price cut over five years is
a genuine open question, not a directional call I can make from the current
reporting — both companies have also committed large domestic manufacturing
investment ($27bn LLY, $10bn NVO) that reads as a bet the volume math works.

**Energy and grid capacity — the binding constraint on the AI trade that
isn't capital.** JLL and Data Center Knowledge describe 2026 as a
power-constrained supercycle: average grid-connection wait times in primary
data-center markets now exceed four years, and Gartner is quoted forecasting
that power shortages will restrict 40% of AI data centers by 2027 ([JLL,
2026](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-outlook/data-center-outlook);
Gartner via industry press, 2026). This is a direct tailwind for VRT
(cooling/power distribution) and ETN (electrification equipment), and
arguably for CAT to the extent it sells backup and on-site generation
equipment into data centers. It is a risk to the monetization timeline for
MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN and META: capex committed does not equal compute deployed
if it cannot be plugged into a grid, which could keep chip shipments (NVDA,
AVGO) strong even as the buyers' own AI return-on-capital story slips.

**China growth and the property drag — a slow-moving, largely anticipated
headwind.** China set a 2026 GDP growth target of 4.5%-5%, its lowest in
decades, with the property sector's total drag on growth (including
spillovers) still estimated at roughly 1.5 percentage points, down from 2
points in 2025 ([CNN,
2026-03-04](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/04/business/china-npc-gdp-economy-intl-hnk);
IMF via ICIS, 2026-02-19). This is a structural, mostly-consensus headwind
for 0700.HK (Tencent) and 9988.HK (Alibaba) domestic revenue, and an
indirect one for LVMUY (Chinese luxury demand) and NVO/LLY's China growth
optionality.

**The dollar — genuinely unclear from current sources, flagged rather than
called.** One data point has the DXY at ~100.2 on 2026-09-18, up 2.63% over
the trailing year ([TradingEconomics via search,
accessed 2026-09-20](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/currency)); a
separate contemporaneous narrative track describes "dollar debasement" easing
pressure on emerging-market currencies (Bloomberg/Yahoo Finance,
2026-08-25). A hiking Fed and a 5% long rate are, mechanically, dollar-
supportive, which would matter for 0700.HK, 9988.HK, 005930.KS and LVMUY
(non-USD revenue translated back). I could not reconcile the two narratives
from the sources gathered and would treat the dollar's direction over the
next year as unresolved rather than call it either way.

## Concentration: how much of this watchlist is one trade

The watchlist has 37 equity tickers (excluding the fund sleeve). Counting
every name whose near-term earnings are directly a function of AI
data-center capital spending — chip designers and foundry/equipment/memory
suppliers (NVDA, AMD, AVGO, TSM, ASML, AMAT, MU, ARM — 8 tickers), the
hyperscalers doing the spending (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META — 4), the
enterprise software names whose growth case rests on AI adoption riding that
infrastructure (CRM, NOW, SNOW — 3), and the power/cooling infrastructure
names that sell directly into the buildout (VRT, ETN — 2) — comes to **17 of
37 tickers, roughly 46% of the equity watchlist**, before adding Samsung
(005930.KS), which is a memory/HBM supplier to the same buildout and would
make it 18 (49%), or CAT to the extent its power-generation and construction
equipment segments serve data centers, which would push it higher still.

These are not 17 independent bets. They are the same trade observed at four
points in one supply chain: design and manufacture the chip (NVDA, AMD,
AVGO, ARM license the architecture; TSM fabricates; ASML and AMAT make the
tools; MU and 005930.KS supply memory) → buy and deploy the chip (MSFT,
GOOGL, AMZN, META) → build software on top of the deployed capacity (CRM,
NOW, SNOW) → supply the power and cooling the deployment physically needs
(VRT, ETN). A slowdown in hyperscaler capex growth — whether from the rate
shock raising the cost of the debt funding it (Force 2 above), from the
grid-capacity ceiling making further spending unprofitable, or from a demand
disappointment in AI products themselves — would not hit one name, it would
hit correlated exposure across roughly half the watchlist simultaneously.
The portfolio-construction question this raises (position sizing, not stock
selection) is squarely outside this brief's remit, but the fact itself is a
finding: what looks like 17-18 diversified names is functionally a handful
of independent bets sized 17-18 times over.

## Priced in versus not, and where I cannot tell

**Likely priced in:** the September rate hike itself — it was well
telegraphed (CNBC ran "odds just shot higher" on 2026-09-14, WSJ ran "all
eyes on Fed" on 2026-09-11, the vote was 12-0) — so the 25bp move itself is
not new information. AI hyperscaler capex growth into 2027 is also
widely-covered consensus (Goldman Sachs, S&P Global, and BofA all publish
similar $1-1.3tn 2027 figures), so the spending trajectory itself is not a
surprise. China's growth slowdown and property drag are targeted and
forecast by Beijing and the IMF openly, so that is consensus too.

**Likely not fully priced in:** the specific claim that the AI financing
circle "just hit its first stress test" from the rate hike (Inkl,
2026-09-2026) is a fresh framing as of this week, not an established
narrative — if credit spreads on AI-linked debt widen in the coming months
that would be new information the market has not digested. The Medicaid
all-50-states MFN news (2026-09-18) is two days old at the time of this
brief and may not be reflected in LLY/NVO guidance yet.

**Cannot tell from available sources:** whether the move in the 10-year to
5% has already been reflected in the multiples the market assigns to the
longest-duration names here (SNOW, ARM, ENPH). That requires current
forward-multiple data by ticker, which is outside a macro sweep — the
company-level analysis should check current P/E and EV/Sales against
pre-hike levels rather than assume either that it is priced in or that a
further de-rating is coming.

## Second-order effects worth flagging

- **Tariff arithmetic can make headline "access restored" news
  overstate the earnings benefit.** The 25% chip tariff plus a 50% volume
  cap on H200/MI325X sales to China means the "Nvidia and AMD can sell to
  China again" headline (2026, various) nets out to a much smaller earnings
  contribution than the pre-ban China business once was — the tax and cap
  are structural, not transitional.
- **Chip shipments can keep growing even if AI's return on capital
  disappoints**, because the grid bottleneck decouples "chips shipped" from
  "chips generating a return." That would keep NVDA/AVGO/TSM revenue
  numbers healthy for longer than hyperscaler capex discipline would
  otherwise suggest, and could be the leading indicator that diverges first
  when the cycle turns — watch hyperscaler capex guidance and utilization
  commentary, not just semiconductor bookings.
- **The oil spike and the Fed's hawkishness are feeding each other.** A
  Hormuz-driven oil shock raises headline inflation, which supports the
  case for the second hike the Fed has already signaled, which raises the
  discount rate further — a channel connecting a geopolitical event
  directly to equity multiples that is easy to miss if oil and rates are
  read as separate stories.
- **GLP-1 pricing cuts and Medicare coverage expansion move in opposite
  directions on revenue** and the net is not obviously negative for LLY/NVO
  despite the "price cut" framing — this is worth modeling explicitly rather
  than assumed as a headwind.

## Three questions I could not answer, and what would answer them

1. **Is the 5% 10-year already reflected in the multiples of the
   longest-duration names (SNOW, ARM, ENPH, TSLA), or is a further
   de-rating still coming?** Would need current forward P/E and EV/Sales for
   each against their own pre-hike (pre-2026-09-16) levels, which is a
   company-level, not macro-level, question.
2. **How exposed is each individual hyperscaler (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META) to
   the Nvidia-OpenAI-Oracle circular financing complex specifically**, as
   opposed to funding capex from its own balance sheet and operating cash
   flow? The aggregate $745bn figure is not broken out by counterparty.
   Would need each company's latest 10-Q financing and related-party
   disclosures.
3. **Does the Strait of Hormuz disruption resolve or escalate?** This
   determines whether the current oil price and inflation shock is
   transitory, as past Middle East spikes have generally been, or a
   structural repricing of energy costs that keeps the Fed hawkish for
   longer than currently signaled. Would need actual shipping-volume data
   through the Strait and a ceasefire or escalation signal, not just the
   spot price.
